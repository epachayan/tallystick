## T9 — Non-attributive adjudication does not survive key compromise

M22 and M23 are evidential twins, verified **25/25 byte-identical** once ground truth is excluded
from the adjudicator's view. In both, the committed record contains an out-of-scope action signed
with the executor's key, and the executor claims the key was compromised.

- **M22:** the executor performed the action and the compromise claim is a lie.
- **M23:** a thief holding the compromised key performed it. The executor is honest.

*Did you do it, or did the thief?* Nothing in the evidence answers this.

| baseline | M22 (guilty, false excuse) | M23 (honest, thief used key) |
|---|---|---|
| B7 verifiable adjudication | correct blame 25/25 | **false accusation 25/25** |
| B7r as deployed under a revocation claim | **missed 25/25** | spurious 25/25 |
| B10 composed (non-attributive) | correct contradiction 25/25 | **false accusation 25/25** |
| B15 short-term irrevocable keys | correct contradiction 25/25 | **false accusation 25/25** |

### The result

> **T9.** The non-attributive escape from T4 does not survive key compromise. `contradicted` is a
> claim about **the key**, not the person — and when the key has been stolen, binding the key
> binds the wrong party.

This is the sharpest limit found on B9/B10. Their whole value (T6) was avoiding over-attribution by
refusing to speak about persons. But *"your commitment says X"* is only a statement about a
record while the key and the person coincide. Key compromise severs them, and the
non-attributive vocabulary silently re-attaches.

**Consequence for the paper:** B10's Pareto dominance (T6) holds only under an unstated assumption —
that a party's key is a faithful proxy for the party. That assumption should be added to the ledger
and stated wherever T6 is claimed.

## The excuse hierarchy, measured

K5 predicted that a rational adversary occupies the excuse that is both indistinguishable from
honest misfortune and maximally destructive of evidence. Measured across the corpus:

| excuse | provable? | destroys prior evidence? | best baseline result |
|---|---|---|---|
| tamper, produce record (M4) | **yes** — fails verification | no | caught 25/25 |
| tamper, claim record lost (M19) | no | no | **missed by every baseline** |
| tamper, claim key compromised (M22) | no | **yes, retroactively** | missed by B7r as deployed |

The ranking is confirmed. Claiming loss beats tampering; claiming compromise beats claiming loss,
because revocation voids evidence that already exists rather than merely withholding evidence that
would have.

## B15 removes the excuse — and hands the bill to the honest party

Short-term irrevocable keys work exactly as the 2002 survey describes: a per-run key issued by the
party under its long-term key, with a stated lifetime and no revocation path. A signature inside a
completed run cannot be voided later, so the excuse is unavailable.

It works. B15 recovers full adjudication on M22 where B7r misses entirely.

**And it false-accuses on M23 25/25.** Removing the excuse means the genuinely compromised party is
bound to every signature made inside the compromise window — including the thief's.

> **T8, confirmed a sixth time and from a sixth mechanism.** The excuse could not be eliminated,
> only re-denominated: from *undetected misconduct* (the excuse works) into *the honest
> key-compromise victim bearing the thief's actions*.

The 2002 authors are explicit that this is the trade — the short lifetime makes the risk
"acceptable," not absent. That is a deliberate choice of currency, matching every other family
surveyed.

## Caveats

- The compromise window is not modelled as a duration. B15 is scored as removing the excuse
  outright; a real deployment would need the window measured against the dispute-arrival rate.
- No key hierarchy, no certificate chain, no time-stamping authority — the three countermeasure
  families in the 2002 survey. Only the second is modelled.
- M23 assumes the thief acts within scope of what the key permits. A thief who also forges the
  authorization is a different and probably harder class.
