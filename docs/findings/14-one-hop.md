# 14 — One hop: composition, localisation, and the general boundary

**Date:** 15 October 2026 · **Opens:** the multi-hop phase (RESEARCH §13)
**Corpus:** 225 three-party scenarios, 9 H-classes, separate from and not changing the two-party
corpus · **Figures:** `results/multihop.txt` (regenerated by `make experiments`)

The two-party study fixed one delegation between two parties. This finding adds one intermediary:
a principal P delegates to an intermediary I, which sub-delegates to an executor E, and I reports
the execution back to P. Nothing else changes: same scorer semantics, same metrics module, same
disclosure policies, and the two-party figures are pinned by `src/multihop/freeze.py` so none of
this can move them.

The question is what the two-party results become when the party a principal deals with is not the
party that acts.

---

## How the phase is built

Each protocol is now two things kept apart: an **interaction-protocol specification** in
`specs/*.toml` (which messages are signed, which commitments each binds, which earlier messages it
carries, what an honest receiver checks) and an **adjudicator** (what it concludes from the copies
produced). The harness derives every signed copy, and who holds it, from the specification. See
[`../protocols/README.md`](../protocols/README.md).

Three specifications:

| spec | what it is |
|---|---|
| `bearer3` | bearer tokens and self-written logs; nothing binds anyone |
| `hop3` | every hop signed and acknowledged; the intermediary **re-signs** its own report |
| `chain3` | the intermediary **forwards** E's signatures to P, and each receipt binds the commitment it was issued under |

`B13_hop3` and `B13_chain3` run the **same adjudicator code**. Every difference between them is
caused by what the protocol carries.

## Results

| protocol | spec | full-class | localised | false attribution | false contradiction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0_bearer3 | bearer3 | 3/9 full-class | 0/8 localised | 0 | 0 |
| B1_hop3 | hop3 | 5/9 full-class | 3/8 localised | 75 | 0 |
| B13_hop3 | hop3 | 6/9 full-class | 3/8 localised | 0 | 0 |
| B13_chain3 | chain3 | 8/9 full-class | 6/8 localised | 0 | 0 |
| B17_chain3 | chain3 | 6/9 full-class | 4/8 localised | 0 | 50 |

*Localised* is new and stricter than correct: the verdict names exactly the parties whose account
diverges, D(w). Correctness accepts any non-empty subset of D, which is right for a claim about the
record. It is not the same as telling a principal where in its chain the fault lies. The
denominator is the number of classes with a non-empty D.

### 1. The witness adjudicator composes across a hop without modification

`B13_chain3` reaches 8/9 full-class with no false attribution and no false contradiction, using the
two-party recomputation rule unchanged: each party's assertion is recomputed against every root a
counterparty signed. Nothing about the rule was specific to two parties.

### 2. Re-signing creates a "who" twin; forwarding removes it

H4 and H5 differ in **which** party diverged. In H4, E overreached and went silent, and I has
genuinely lost E's acknowledgement. In H5, I widened the sub-grant, withheld E's acknowledgement
and called it lost, and E acted in good faith but is unreachable.

Under `hop3` the two worlds project to the same evidence. P holds only I's signatures, and the
evidence that would separate them is E's acknowledgement, which nobody produces. D is {E} in one
and {I} in the other, so no verdict is correct in both. `B13_hop3` misses both, and `B1_hop3` blames
the silent party and is wrong in H5.

Under `chain3` they are not twins. P holds E's receipt, and the receipt binds the sub-grant it was
executed under. `B13_chain3` contradicts E in H4 and I in H5, and localises both.

Stated plainly: **when an intermediary restates what happened downstream, a principal cannot tell a
dishonest intermediary from a dishonest executor. When it forwards the executor's own signatures, it
can.**

### 3. Collusion is asymmetric: witnesses flow downstream early and upstream late

- **Upstream collusion (H8).** After the fact, P and I re-sign a narrower grant to pin an
  authorised action on E. Under `hop3`, the adjudicator still contradicts I, whose sub-grant to E
  is held by E. That verdict is correct, but P's part goes unseen. Under `chain3`, E also holds P's
  original signature over the grant, forwarded with the sub-grant, so P has signed two versions and
  both colluders are named.
- **Downstream collusion (H6).** I and E agree a cover story before anything reaches P. No protocol
  in the set handles it. Every signature P receives was issued over the cover version, and its twin
  (H7, where P sincerely misattributes an outside effect) projects to identical evidence.

The asymmetry is structural. Under `chain3`, P's signature travels downstream at delegation time,
so a downstream honest party holds evidence before any dispute exists. E's signature reaches P only
at report time, after colluders have already agreed what to sign. **Forwarding protects the
downstream party from upstream collusion, and not the reverse.** Closing H6 needs evidence P obtains
independently of the chain, at execution time: an attestor, or a receipt E sends to P directly. That
is a protocol change, and it is not attempted here.

### 4. The two-party boundary was the special case; the general rule is intersection

Finding 12 stated: a twin pair is escapable iff D(guilty) == D(honest). That rule was derived when
every D was a subset of {P, E}. The statement underneath it is more general: a pair is escapable iff
some verdict is scored correct in both worlds. Reading that off the scorer:

> **escapable iff D(a) ∩ D(b) is non-empty (contradict the overlap), or abstention is correct in
> both worlds (nobody dishonest, every divergence a mistake).**

H8/H9 separates the two rules. D is {P, I} in one and {I} in the other. The equality rule says the
pair binds. In fact `B13_hop3` handles both worlds by contradicting I, which is true in both.

`src/multihop/boundary.py` does not rely on either rule. It enumerates every verdict the vocabulary
can express and scores it against both worlds. The intersection rule reproduces that brute force on
all four three-party twin pairs, and on all ten two-party twin pairs, where it also agrees with the
classification in finding 12. The two rules coincide when there are two parties, which is why the
narrower rule was never contradicted there.

The intersection rule also makes one edge explicit that the plain-language form of finding 12
skipped. Take a world where nothing diverged and a world where every divergence is a sincere
mistake. These differ in *whether* a divergence occurred, yet abstention is correct in both, so the
pair is escapable. Neither corpus contains such a pair, so finding 12's wording was never
contradicted. It was, however, broader than the scorer supports.

The equality rule survives as something else, and the second boundary is just as short:

> **A twin pair can be *localised* in both worlds iff D(a) == D(b).**

One verdict names one set, and localising means that set is D in both worlds. So with two parties,
correctness and localisation share a boundary. With three they come apart. H8/H9 is the pair where
the right answer is available but the complete answer is not.

As with finding 12, these are one-line arguments once stated, and they should not be presented as
deep theorems. The contribution is that both are checked by a build stage against brute force and
against the harness.

### 5. A duty to answer, extended to a third party, frames the honest

`B17_chain3` adds the two-party duty to answer, unmodified: a party whose signature is in evidence
must produce its record, and a party shown to have received a message must produce it. It scores
6/9 full-class, against 8/9 for `B13_chain3` without the duty, and introduces 50 false
contradictions:

- **H4.** I genuinely lost its own copy of E's acknowledgement. P's forwarded copy proves the
  message existed, so the duty contradicts I, who is honest.
- **H5.** E is honest and simply unreachable. Its signature is in evidence, so the duty contradicts
  E.

In finding 10 the duty traded misses for false contradictions one-for-one. Here it buys nothing and
costs two classes. The duty presumes that the party is in the dispute and able to answer. An
executor behind an intermediary often is neither.

### 6. A specification check prevents what adjudication can only explain

`chain3`'s sub-grant carries P's signed grant, and an honest E checks that the sub-grant fits inside
it before acting. Derived from the specification and diffed against the declaration in
`src/multihop/conformance.py`, that check refuses H2, H3, H5 and H9 before any out-of-delegation
action is executed. The corpus is held fixed across specifications, so the harness still
adjudicates those worlds as they happened. Prevention is reported separately and is not scored.

### 7. Cost

The median disclosure is 1061 bytes for `B13_hop3` and 1521 for `B13_chain3`, about 43% more,
paid in forwarded signatures and roots. The byte accounting counts a signature and a root per
copy, which differs from the two-party accounting, so these figures do not compare with
`results/canonical.txt`. Only one hop was measured, and no growth rate across hops is claimed.

## A defect the derivation caught

The first version of the fabrication rule let colluders swap only copies they both signed and held.
That left P's acknowledgement of the report signed over the original report in H6, a report P never
received. The twin audit derives twins from projected views rather than accepting the declaration,
and it reported H6/H7 as twins under `bearer3` only, against a declaration of all three
specifications. The rule was wrong, not the declaration: a cover story agreed before the report
means every party, honest ones included, only ever saw the cover version. Corrected in
`src/multihop/evidence.py` and pinned by a test. It is the same shape as the six declaration
defects in RESEARCH §9a: reasoning about the model missed a case that deriving from it found.

## Limits

- **One hop, linear.** No fan-out, no second intermediary, no agent joining mid-chain.
- **The classes were written to probe stated predictions.** Most per-class results confirm that the
  mechanism behaves as reasoned. What is independent of the author's expectations is the
  brute-force boundary check, the cross-check against the two-party pairs, the derived twin and
  prevention audits, and the defect above.
- **The forum is assumed.** The adjudicator receives every copy any party produces, including E's,
  even though E is not a party to P's dispute with I. Where no such forum exists, `hop3` and
  `chain3` differ more than measured here, since only `chain3` puts E's signatures in P's hands.
- **Availability is all-or-nothing per party, and withholding is per message.** There is no
  transport model, no timing, and no fair exchange, the same limits as the two-party phase.
- **Prevalence is unmeasured (N1),** exactly as before.
