## T7 — T1 is scale-invariant

Statefulness was the most promising evidential escape from T5, and it had a reason behind it:
T4's criterion says mistake is separable from malice exactly when malice requires a
**concealing act**. A single claim of loss is an *assertion*. A pattern of claimed losses looked
like a *behaviour* — and behaviours leave traces.

M20 and M21 are pattern twins. M20 is an executor repeatedly claiming loss to hide repeated
tampering. M21 is an executor suffering genuine repeated loss **at the same observed rate**.

| class | B10 (stateless) | B11 (stateful) |
|---|---|---|
| M19 single-dispute refuge | missed 25/25 | missed 25/25 |
| M20 repeat offender | missed 25/25 | **correct 18/25** |
| M21 unlucky honest | correct 25/25 | **false accusation 23/25** |

**Statefulness does not escape T5. It converts a miss into a false accusation.**

That is precisely T1's dichotomy — accuracy or completeness, not both — reappearing at the
level of *rates* rather than single disputes. The honest-but-unlucky executor and the
disciplined offender have overlapping observed distributions, so no threshold separates them.

> **T7. Moving from single-dispute evidence to pattern evidence does not escape the
> accuracy/completeness dichotomy. It reinstates it one level up.**

This is a stronger and cleaner result than T5 alone, and it is the one I would build the paper
around, because it forecloses the obvious objection ("just look at the pattern") with a
measurement rather than an argument.

## What statefulness does buy: a bound, not an escape

One-sided binomial test, honest base loss rate `p₀ = 0.25`, `α = 0.05`:

| n disputes | adversary rate | detection power | false accusation on honest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 0.30 | 0.113 | 0.041 |
| 20 | 0.40 | 0.404 | 0.041 |
| 20 | 0.60 | **0.943** | 0.041 |
| 50 | 0.40 | 0.664 | 0.029 |
| 50 | 0.60 | **0.999** | 0.029 |

A greedy adversary is caught reliably. A disciplined one is not — but discipline costs it:

| n | max rate staying under 50% detection | misconduct per 100 disputes |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.54 | 54 |
| 20 | 0.42 | 42 |
| 50 | 0.37 | 37 |
| 100 | 0.32 | 32 |

> **An adversary that stays below the detection threshold has capped its own misconduct at
> roughly the honest base failure rate.** It cannot hide unlimited concealment — only
> concealment indistinguishable from bad luck.

That is the honest statement of what pattern evidence achieves. Not attribution. Not deterrence.
**Rate limiting**, converging on `p₀` as the observation window grows.

And the price is paid by honest parties: at `α = 0.05` roughly 3–4% of genuinely unlucky
executors are wrongly flagged, forever, and no choice of `α` removes the trade — it only moves
it along the same curve.

## Consequences

**T5 survives, correctly scoped.** Not "within a purely evidential framework" as originally
written, but the stronger claim now backed by T7: no evidential framework escapes it, at any
level of aggregation, because each level reproduces T1.

**Bonding is now genuinely forced, and for a better reason than before.** The earlier argument
was that T5 left a consequence-free refuge. The argument now is that *every* evidential
response to that refuge reproduces T1's dichotomy, so the escape cannot be evidential at all.
An economic mechanism does not need to distinguish the unlucky from the disciplined — it prices
the ambiguity for both. That is a structural escape, not a better detector.

**A4 is resolved.** The adjudicator is stateful. That was already required by B6's oracle
defence, and B11 makes it explicit. The follow-on question is now open and specific: a stateful
adjudicator accumulates a cross-dispute profile of both parties, which is itself a disclosure
channel that none of the byte-count measurements capture.

**The through-line held again.** Every earlier fix worked by making a trusted party accountable.
This one failed — and it failed because the thing being made accountable was *bad luck*, which
has no agent to hold responsible. That is a useful boundary on the pattern: it works on parties,
not on outcomes.

## Caveats

- `p₀ = 0.25` is invented. The real rate of genuine record loss is unmeasured — the same problem
  as A1, and it determines the entire operating point.
- The adversary model is static: it picks a rate and holds it. A rate-adaptive adversary that
  tracks its own p-value would do better, and is untested.
- The binomial test assumes independent disputes. Correlated failure (one outage causing many
  losses) would inflate false accusations further and is unmodelled.
