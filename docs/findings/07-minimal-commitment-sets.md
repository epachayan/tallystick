## The construction

T4 said: mistake is separable from malice exactly when malice requires a **concealing act**. We
had used it to classify existing protocols. Inverted, it becomes a design question:

> Given a set of dishonest behaviours, what is the **smallest set of commitments** such that every
> one becomes concealment-requiring rather than assertional?

Each commitment converts one of a party's future assertions from *something it can simply say* into
*something it must contradict*. The 2001 transparent-TTP protocol placed `h(k)` in the signed
evidence precisely so that substituting a key becomes a provable contradiction — this rule already
in use, unnamed.

## The answer, for this corpus

Seven commitment types were available. **Four suffice**, covering all 14 bindable adversarial
classes:

| commitment | binds |
|---|---|
| `C_auth` — principal signs the granted scope | M3, M5, M6, M8, M9, M11, M12, M13 |
| `C_exec` — executor signs its execution record | M1, M2, M4, M18 |
| `C_recv` — principal signs acknowledgement of delivery | M7 |
| `C_witness` — messages carry public correctness witnesses | M14 |

Three commitments are **not needed**: `C_retain`, `C_key`, `C_hist`. That is not an accident —
each was aimed at the residual, and none of them binds anything.

---

> **CRITERION SUPERSEDED, 11 August 2026 (RC-H12).** Bindability here is assessed
> **per class**, and that is the wrong unit. `C_retain` — already in the
> vocabulary below — does force M19 and M20 into contradiction, and `B17` implements
> exactly it. But it convicts their honest twins (M10, M21) at the same rate.
>
> The criterion is now **pairwise**: an act is bindable iff some commitment forces
> it into contradiction *without contradicting its evidentially identical honest
> twin*. Under that criterion the residual below is correct again, for a sharper
> reason — and it coincides exactly with the ESCAPABLE/BINDS boundary of
> `docs/findings/12-conservation-boundary.md`. **A class is bindable iff its twin
> pair is escapable.** The minimal-commitment-set analysis and the conservation
> boundary are the same result in two vocabularies.

## T10 — The residual is non-empty, and it is exactly the excuse family

Three adversarial classes admit **no** binding commitment:

| class | claim | why unbindable |
|---|---|---|
| **M19** | "my record is unavailable" | Loss postdates every commitment and is unobservable to the counterparty. `C_retain` promises production, but production requires the artifact still to exist. |
| **M22** | "my key was compromised" | A party cannot pre-commit that its key will not be stolen. `C_key` bounds the *window*; it does not make the claim false. |
| **M20** | "I lose records at this rate" | No single commitment fixes a statistical property of a party's own future behaviour. |

### The characterisation

> **T10.** A dishonest act is bindable iff its false assertion concerns a **particular fact that
> existed at commitment time**. It is residual iff the assertion concerns either an **event
> postdating every commitment** (loss, compromise) or an **aggregate property of the party's own
> future behaviour** (a rate).
>
> **You cannot commit to a proposition about your own future misfortune.**

This is a stronger impossibility than T1. T1 says a two-valued adjudicator cannot be both accurate
and complete on a given indistinguishable pair. T10 says there is a class of dishonest acts that
**no amount of protocol design can make attributable**, because the commitments that would bind
them are unmakeable in principle rather than merely absent.

### It explains the excuse hierarchy

Findings 06 measured that claiming key compromise beats claiming record loss beats tampering. T10
supplies the reason: **the best excuses are exactly the unbindable ones.** An adversary does not
need to find a clever lie; it needs to find a claim about a fact that postdates the commitments.

## Verification, and a correction it forced

The naive test — *did any baseline get the residual classes right?* — reported that M19 and M22
were both handled 25/25, apparently refuting T10.

That was the wrong test. Those "catches" come from schemes that blame **every** non-producer, which
also condemns the honest twin. The correct test is paired:

> Can any baseline get the adversarial class right **without** failing its honest twin?

| adversarial | honest twin | class | baselines succeeding |
|---|---|---|---|
| M6 narrowed scope | M15 misremembered | bindable | B9, B10, B11, B15 |
| M1 overreach | M16 stale grant | bindable | B9, B10, B11, B15 |
| M7 denied receipt | M17 misremembered | bindable | B9, B10, B11, B15 |
| **M19** claimed loss | M10 genuine loss | **residual** | **NONE** |
| **M20** claimed rate | M21 genuine rate | **residual** | **NONE** |
| **M22** claimed compromise | M23 genuine theft | **residual** | **NONE** |

**Every bindable class is separable. No residual class is.** Prediction and measurement agree
under the paired test, across twelve baselines.

### The correction

M20 was initially classified as **bindable** via `C_hist` — the adjudicator retaining cross-dispute
history. The paired test refuted it, and the reason sharpens the theory:

> **`C_hist` is not a commitment.** It is an adjudicator-side *observation*. A party cannot
> contradict a record it never made. Only party-made commitments bind.

This is independently confirmed by T7: statefulness converted a miss into a false accusation rather
than binding anything — exactly what a non-commitment would do.

That distinction — *party-made commitment* versus *observer-held record* — is the sharpest thing to
come out of this analysis, and it was found by a failed prediction.

| result | status |
|---|---|
| **T10** | New, and the strongest impossibility in the project. Verified across 12 baselines under the paired test. |
| **T4** | **Promoted from diagnostic to constructive.** It now yields a design procedure and a minimality result, not just a classification. |
| **T5 / findings 06** | **Explained.** The excuse hierarchy is the residual set, ordered by destructiveness. |
| **T8** | **Explained at the level of cause.** Conservation holds *because* the residual is non-empty. Where nothing can be bound, cost can only be moved. |

**T8 and T10 now fit together as one story.** T10 says a residual exists that no commitment can
bind. T8 says the cost of that residual can only be denominated, not removed. The first is the
cause; the second is the consequence.

## Caveats

- The commitment vocabulary is enumerated by hand, not derived. A commitment type outside the seven
  might bind a residual class — T10 is a claim about *this* vocabulary until the space is
  characterised properly.
- The residual characterisation ("facts postdating commitment, or aggregate properties") is
  proposed from three instances. It predicts correctly here, but three is not many.
- **The obvious attack on T10:** external attestation. A hardware root of trust or a third-party
  monitor could in principle testify that a record existed or a key was not compromised — moving
  the residual rather than eliminating it, since the attestor then becomes the unbindable party.
  Worth building as **B16** before T10 is claimed in print.

## Next

1. **B16 — external attestation** against the residual classes. The strongest challenge to T10, and
   the prediction is that it relocates the residual to the attestor rather than closing it.
2. **Characterise the commitment space** rather than enumerating it, so T10 becomes a claim about
   all commitments and not seven of them.
3. ~~Ablation~~ — **done.** Dropping any one of the four leaves acts unbound, so the set is minimal:

   | dropped | acts left unbound |
   |---|---|

   | `C_exec` | 4 — M1, M2, M4, M18 |
   | `C_recv` | 1 — M7 |
   | `C_witness` | 1 — M14 |

   No commitment is redundant. `C_auth` carries most of the load, which matches intuition: the
   signed grant is what nearly every principal-side lie must contradict.
