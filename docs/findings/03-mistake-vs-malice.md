## T4 — Evidence establishes contradiction, not culpability

### The result

M15, M16 and M17 are **evidential twins** of M6, M1 and M7: generated from the same seed
stream, identical in every respect except the party's internal state. The malicious version
lies; the mistake version sincerely misremembers or acts in good faith on a stale grant.

| malicious | mistake | adjudicator views identical | situation |
|---|---|---|---|
| M6 | M15 | **25/25** | principal asserts a narrower scope |
| M7 | M17 | **25/25** | principal denies receiving the result |
| M1 | M16 | 0/25 | executor acts outside the granted scope |

For M6/M15 and M7/M17 the adjudicator's view is **byte-identical**. Any function of that view
returns the same verdict on both. Therefore no evidence-based scheme can distinguish a lying
principal from a mistaken one — and every scheme that emits `exposed` is asserting bad faith it
cannot support.

**B7 — the baseline that scored perfectly in Experiment 02 and survived worst-case adversarial
selection — accuses a sincerely mistaken party in 100 of 475 scenarios.** It was never wrong
about the record. It was wrong about the person, in every mistake scenario, silently.

### The boundary — and this is the sharper half

M1/M16 does **not** match, and that is the interesting row.

- **M6/M15, M7/M17 match** because the malicious version requires only an *assertion*: claim a
  different scope, deny a receipt. A liar and someone who misremembers perform the identical
  speech act.
- **M1/M16 differs** because the malicious version requires an act of *concealment*: the liar
  omits the action from its own record, while the good-faith actor on a stale grant reports what
  it did. Concealment leaves a trace.

> **Mistake is separable from malice exactly when malice requires an additional concealing act.
> Where malice is purely assertional, no evidence-based scheme can attribute fault.**

That is a clean, testable criterion, and it tells a designer where to look: if you want
attributable fault, structure the protocol so that lying requires concealment rather than mere
assertion. Nothing in the surveyed literature states this.

### B9 — non-attributive adjudication

B9 emits `{contradicted, unresolved, clear}`. `contradicted` is a claim about the **record** —
*this assertion conflicts with your commitment* — and makes no claim about the person.

| | correct_contradiction | over_attribution | false_accusation | missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B9 | **425** | 0 | 0 | 50 |

It detects everything B7 detects at the record level. It simply stops short of saying why. The
50 missed are M12 (nothing was ever committed) and M14 (adjudicator collusion) — B9 as written
inspects records only, so a corrupt adjudicator is outside its reach. **Composing B9's verdict
vocabulary with B7's public transcript is the obvious next baseline and should be trivial.**

## Why this matters beyond the harness

The 2026 agentic-governance literature promises *attribution*: irrefutable evidence tying an
action to a responsible party. T4 says evidence-based accountability delivers something
strictly weaker — that a claim conflicts with a commitment. Deciding whether that conflict is a
lie, a lapse of memory, a stale cache, or a misunderstanding is a **separate step, and
cryptography cannot perform it**.

That is not a philosophical caveat. It has a concrete consequence: any system that
auto-escalates from "record contradicted" to "party at fault" — suspension, penalty, contract
termination — will punish honest mistakes at whatever rate mistakes occur. Given that human
principals routinely misremember authorizations granted weeks earlier, that rate is not small.

**The honest claim for a paper:** *cryptographic accountability establishes contradiction;
attribution of fault requires evidence cryptography cannot supply, except where the protocol
forces concealment.*

## Caveats

- **Novelty unverified.** The distinction between contradiction and culpability is old in law
  (mens rea) and in fault-tolerance (Byzantine covers both mistake and malice by refusing to
  distinguish them). What appears new is stating it as a *design criterion* for delegation
  protocols, plus the concealment boundary. Check the accountability and BFT literature before
  claiming either.
- **The mistake classes are constructed, not observed.** No data supports the claim that human
  principals misremember at a meaningful rate. It is plausible and it matches ordinary
  experience, but it is an assumption and should be labelled as one.
- **B9 is not deployable as written.** It abstains on adjudicator collusion. Compose with B7.
